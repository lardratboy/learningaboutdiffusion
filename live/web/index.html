What you are looking at

ε̂∅, ε̂ = UNet(z_t, t, [negative, prompt]) ε̂_cfg = ε̂∅ + w · (ε̂ − ε̂∅) x̂₀ = (z_t − √(1−ᾱ_t) · ε̂_cfg) / √ᾱ_t z_next = scheduler.step(z_t, ε̂_cfg)
z_t: the noisy latent (4 × 64 × 64)
At step 0 this is pure Gaussian noise. It is the only state the sampler carries.
ε̂∅ and ε̂: predicted noise
The UNet's guess of which part of z_t is noise. ε̂∅ comes from the negative prompt and ε̂ from your prompt. Both run in one batched pass.
ε̂ − ε̂∅: what the prompt changes
Tiny compared with ε̂ itself (it is auto-scaled), but it is the whole signal that steers the image.
x̂₀: the model's current guess of the final latent
Decoded below as an image. Watch layout settle early and detail arrive late.
cross-attention maps: one tile per prompt token
Where in the image each word is being "read", averaged over every head of all 16 cross-attention layers and resampled to 32 × 32.
prompt embedding: CLIP output, one 768-vector per token
Fixed for the whole run. Each vector is folded into a 24 × 32 tile.
timestep embedding and UNet bottleneck
How t is fed into the network, and the first 64 of 1,280 channels at its narrowest 8 × 8 point.
Compared with the walk-through diffusion-walk.html teaches the modern recipe: a DiT transformer, rectified flow, velocity prediction. SD 1.5 is the earlier one. A UNet (conv + attention) predicts noise ε on a variance-preserving schedule:
walk-through: z_t = (1 − t)·z₀ + t·ε SD 1.5: z_t = √ᾱ_t·z₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t)·ε
So √(1−ᾱ) here plays the role of t there. The latent is 4 × 64 × 64 (8× downsampling), and text enters through cross-attention, not a joint sequence. Classifier-free guidance, x̂₀ and the sampling loop are the same idea.
